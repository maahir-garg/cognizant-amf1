# Product rationale

Impact Lap addresses the ideathon question by turning Aston Martin Aramco's published environmental, social and governance data into a car-led story for fans and a sourced working view for partners. The product has two audiences and one fact base.

## How AI transforms the data

| Input | Transformation | What people see |
|---|---|---|
| Published reports, quotes and page references | Extract and validate facts; preserve status and quality flags | A figure that opens its source, calculation or caveat |
| A fan's optional city, interests and experience level | Select relevant facts and draft a short explanation from only those facts | Context for each stop in the car's route, with inline citations |
| Partner questions about a topic or programme | Draft a post or brief from the same governed facts | A reviewable narrative with citations and checked figures |

The model does not measure team operations. A numeric guardrail rejects figures that do not match a cited fact or derived value; a grounded template supplies copy if generation fails. The offline demo works from cached or template text.

## Fan route

The page opens with approved car imagery and a clear path into the story. Scrolling follows factory preparation, freight, the circuit and work after the race. Each chapter makes one point, then reveals the report figure and its evidence status. A persistent stage guide lets the fan jump around; scrolling never locks. Environment, belonging, community and governance remain visible to every fan.

City and interest choices are optional. A selected city can bring forward a published local example or a supported nearby race; it cannot create a regional figure that has not been reported. The knowledge challenge follows the story and reveals the exact sourced answer after a conceptual choice.

The [Straits Times playground feature](https://www.straitstimes.com/multimedia/graphics/2024/10/sg-playground-culture/index.html) is the main interaction reference: a clear scroll cue, paced visual chapters and evidence close to each claim. [Lando Norris's site](https://landonorris.com/) informs the car-led sense of movement. Neither supplies Impact Lap's data or assets. Official car image sources and reuse notes are in [`public/brand/README.md`](../public/brand/README.md).

## Partner route

The partner view keeps the source trail, data gaps and calculation assumptions visible. It offers a dashboard, scenario controls, exports and grounded drafts. Cognizant's useful role is the data pipeline, fact selection, explanation and review workflow; a person approves any external communication.

## Why the web

A link opens without an app download or account, works for a casual fan on a phone, and can be shared by the team and partners. A focused destination gives the report material room for visual explanation while still linking every claim back to the source. Personal choices stay on the device or in a shareable URL; the core learning path asks for no personal data.

## What is current and what is proposed

The prototype uses the latest published team data available in its fact base. It does not have a connected operational feed or live circuit telemetry. Timely reporting would require source access, data validation, update ownership and publication approval. A personalised driver film is also a separate production idea that needs approved footage, likeness rights and an accessible fallback; it is not part of the current AI transformation.

## Positioning

[AWorld's ActNow work](https://actnow.aworld.org/), [platform](https://www.aworld.org/platform/) and [MotoGP case study](https://www.aworld.org/case-studies/motogp/) show a broad engagement product with learning, challenges and action tracking. Impact Lap's specific contribution is to start with the team's own report evidence, organise it around the car's operations, and reuse the same cited facts for fans and partners. The pitch should describe that focus without claiming AWorld lacks personalisation, reporting or motorsport content.
